Your dasha: the chapter you're in

Most people check the daily stuff: Mercury walking backward, the Moon's sign, what Saturn is doing this month. Underneath that runs a bigger clock. Jyotish splits your life into periods called dashas (Sanskrit for "stage of life"), and each one is run by a single graha (Sanskrit for "the one who grabs": the nine characters in your chart). In Parashara's main system, Vimshottari ("the 120"), the full cycle runs 120 years, and each graha gets its own stretch, from 6 years for the Sun to 20 for Venus (Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra).

The graha whose period you're in is the main character of those years. It sets the theme. Where that graha sits in your chart shows which part of life the theme plays out in.

For one person it's a Saturn period, the auditor's chapter: 19 years of building slowly, a career that rewards patience more than talent
For another it's a Rahu period, the magician's chapter: 18 years of wanting more, new cities, new industries, big swings
For another it's a Jupiter period, the teacher's chapter: 16 years of teachers, study, children and a wider circle

A transit is a few weeks or months of weather inside that chapter. A rainy week in a good chapter is still a good chapter. A sunny week in a hard chapter doesn't change the plot.

Sutra: "Read the chapter before you blame the weather"

Practical remedy: find out which graha's period you're in, and which house that graha sits in. Write one line: "These years are about ___". Check every big decision against that line, and let today's horoscope wait.
