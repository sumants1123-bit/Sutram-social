Who are you?

It's the oldest question, and every tradition gives the same first instruction: know yourself. Sutram is built on that instruction.

Sutram means thread. In the Bhagavad Gita (7.7), Krishna says all of this is strung on him like pearls on a thread. Your birth chart shows the pearls: your body, your mind, your drive, how you think, what brings you joy, and when each of these comes forward. Philosophy points to the thread that runs through all of them.

So this page joins two old instruments. Philosophy brings the question and the method. Jyotish brings the personal detail, read the classical way. Varahamihira calls the Sun the soul of Time and the Moon its mind (Brihat Jataka 2.1). Parashara reads one chart three ways: from the Ascendant for the body, the Moon for the mind and the Sun for the soul (Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra, the Sudarshana chakra). And the Tao Te Ching names the goal: "He who knows himself is intelligent" (chapter 33, James Legge).
