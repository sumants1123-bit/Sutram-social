How a gemstone works

The Rig Veda sings of the Sun's chariot: "Seven Bay Steeds harnessed to thy car bear thee" (1.50.8, tr. Griffith). Many read those seven horses as the seven colours hidden in white light. You are light too: all seven colours, some bright, some faint.

Each graha has its stone (Phaladeepika, chapter 2). Seven colours for seven grahas; Rahu and Ketu, the shadows, take hessonite and cat's eye.

How it works: a stone holds one colour, clean and strong. Wear it, and that colour grows brighter in you. One colour feeds many parts of life: Jupiter's yellow alone looks after wisdom, teachers, children, savings and luck.

Why each stone is known for something:
Diamond for love: Venus, the lover, has many colours, and a diamond flashes every one.
Yellow sapphire for luck: Jupiter, the teacher, is the most generous graha.
Ruby for confidence: the Sun is the king, your self and standing.
Pearl for calm: the Moon is the mind, and a pearl grows slowly in water.
Emerald for words: Mercury rules speech, trade and numbers.
Blue sapphire for discipline: Saturn is work and patience. By custom, people try it for a few days first.

Stones have friends and enemies. The Sun and Saturn are enemies, so ruby and blue sapphire never share a hand (Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra, chapter 3). A stone makes its graha stronger, for good or for trouble, so only your friendly grahas get one. Your rising sign decides who they are (chapter 34).

In real life: gold is a warm light, like the Sun's; silver is a cool light, like the Moon's. Wear an open-back ring so the stone touches your skin, on its graha's finger, starting on its graha's day.
