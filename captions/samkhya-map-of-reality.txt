What is reality made of?

Samkhya, one of the oldest schools of Indian philosophy, answers with a map. Its classic text is the Samkhya Karika of Ishvarakrishna, 72 verses from about 350 CE.

Reality is two. Purusha, the seer: pure awareness that watches and never acts. Prakriti, nature: everything that can be seen, always moving (verses 3, 11, 19).

Nature is woven of three strands, the gunas: sattva (clear), rajas (moving), tamas (heavy). At rest they balance. Near the seer the balance tips and nature unfolds, subtle first (12, 13, 21 to 28, 38):
1. Intellect (buddhi), which decides
2. The I-maker (ahamkara), which says me and mine
3. Mind (manas), which gathers what the senses bring
4. Five senses and five ways to act
5. Five subtle elements: sound, touch, form, taste, smell
6. Five elements: space, air, fire, water, earth
One seer plus 24 layers of nature makes 25.

The mix-up: the seer's light shows in the intellect like a face in a mirror, so thinking seems aware and the seer seems to act (verse 20; the mirror is Vachaspati Mishra's image). Anger rises in the layers, the I-maker calls it mine, the seer only sees it. Samkhya calls this aviveka, not telling the two apart. Advaita Vedanta later named such an error superimposition (adhyasa).

Freedom: as a dancer stops once the audience has seen her, nature stops once fully seen (verse 59). That is kaivalya, the seer standing free.

In your chart: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra (3.20) uses the same word, tattva. Fire is Mars, earth Mercury, space Jupiter, water Venus, air Saturn.

In real life:
Tired body? The last layer
Racing thoughts? The mind
"Why me?" The I-maker
A clear call? The intellect
The one noticing all this? The seer

Samkhya holds that nature is real and that there are many seers. Other maps disagree, and the next parts show them. Next: Yoga's lake of the mind.
