Combust: when your talent stands in someone's shadow

In Jyotish a graha (Sanskrit for "the one who grabs") that sits too close to the Sun is called combust. The old word is asta, "set", the word used for the Sun going down: the graha drops out of view in the Sun's glare. The Surya Siddhanta (chapter 9) gives each graha its distance. Mercury, for example, is lost within about 14 degrees of the Sun, or 12 when it is walking backward.

The Sun is the king, so a combust graha is the person standing right next to the king: close to power, and nobody sees them.

What it can look like in modern life:
Mercury (your words): the idea your boss presents in the meeting
Venus (your taste): you style the room, someone else gets the credit
Mars (your drive): you do the work, a louder person leads
Jupiter (your advice): it sounds better coming from someone else

Right now the messenger (Mercury) is walking backward right beside the king in Libra, so texts, plans and ideas get lost easily. He turns forward on Friday, 13 November.

Sutra: "Too close to the light, no one sees you"

Practical remedy: check which graha sits near your Sun. Do that work under your own name: sign it, post it, present it yourself.
