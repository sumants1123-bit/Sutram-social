The sky that day, 1: the night India won the World Cup

A new series. We take one real event, look at the sky it happened under, and read it backward with the Nadi rules from the daily lessons.

The event. 2 April 2011, Wankhede Stadium, Mumbai. India beat Sri Lanka by six wickets and lifted the World Cup for the first time in 28 years.

The sky. Five grahas (Sanskrit for "the ones who grab") stood in one sign, Pisces: the king (Sun), the soldier (Mars), the mother (Moon), the teacher (Jupiter) and the messenger (Mercury). Straight across, in Virgo, the auditor (Saturn) was walking backward.

Rule 1, same room: grahas in one sign act as one. Read the king as the country, the soldier as the team, the mother as the crowd, the teacher as good counsel and the messenger as the news. Pisces is the teacher's own sign, so they all met in his house.

Rule 4, face to face: the auditor looked straight at all five. Rule 5, looking back: a graha walking backward reopens old files. Together they read as an old account coming up to be settled. For India that was a 28-year wait.

Read together, the whole house gathers in one room, and across the table the auditor closes a 28-year file.

How the timing narrows. The teacher sits in Pisces about one year in twelve. The king and the soldier joined him for three weeks. The mother and the messenger made it five for about two days, and the final fell inside those two days. Slow grahas set the year, fast ones pick the day.

The sky set the scene. The players won it.

Now pick a day from your own life and look at the sky you were under.
